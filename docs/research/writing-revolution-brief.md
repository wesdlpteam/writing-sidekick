# The Writing Revolution and the Hochman Method: research brief

Compiled 3 September 2026 to check how Writing Sidekick's feedback can connect to the writing
framework the school uses. This is a summary in our own words with our own examples. It does not
reproduce any Writing Revolution worksheets, posters or rubrics. THE WRITING REVOLUTION and
THE HOCHMAN METHOD are registered trademarks of The Writing Revolution, Inc.; they are named here
as the source of the ideas only. Sources are listed at the end.

## 1. Core principles

The method rests on six principles (wording confirmed via the Fordham Institute commentary on the book):

1. Students need explicit instruction in writing, beginning in the early elementary grades.
2. Sentences are the building blocks of all writing.
3. When embedded in the content of the curriculum, writing instruction is a powerful teaching tool.
4. The content of the curriculum drives the rigor of the writing activities.
5. Grammar is best taught in the context of student writing.
6. The two most important phases of the writing process are planning and revising.

Hochman and Wexler put the sentence-level premise bluntly: a writer who cannot compose a decent
sentence will never produce a decent essay. Sentence-level work starts wherever the student's skill
actually is, whatever their age.

## 2. Sentence-level strategies (TWR's own names)

The 3-12 strategy list names: Sentences & Fragments, Scrambled Sentences, Sentence Types,
Developing Questions, Basic Conjunctions (because, but, so), Subordinating Conjunctions, Sentence
Expansion, Appositives, Sentence Combining, Transitions.

- **Sentences & Fragments.** Tell a complete sentence from a fragment and fix the fragment.
  Example: "After the bushfire swept through the valley." becomes "After the bushfire swept through
  the valley, the farmers began replanting the paddocks."
- **Scrambled Sentences.** Reorder jumbled words (about 7 to 9) into a punctuated sentence.
- **Sentence Types.** Write a statement, a question, a command and an exclamation about one topic.
- **Developing Questions.** Generate questions about a topic, picture or text before writing.
- **Basic Conjunctions (because, but, so).** Extend one stem three ways to show reason, contrast
  and result. Kernel: "The kangaroo hopped away." because a dingo had appeared / but the joey
  stayed behind / "A dingo appeared, so the kangaroo hopped away."
- **Subordinating Conjunctions.** Complete or write sentences that begin with a subordinating
  conjunction. TWR's Level 1 pacing guide starts with after, before, whenever, even though, since,
  if. Fuller sets group them by meaning: reason (as, because, since), concession (although, even
  though, though, whereas, while), purpose (so that), condition (if, unless), place (where,
  wherever), time (after, before, once, until, when, whenever, while, as soon as).
  Example: "Although the drought had lasted three years, the wheat crop still failed."
- **Sentence Expansion.** Start from a kernel sentence (simple, active, no modifiers) and expand
  it with question words in this order: who, what, when, where, why, how (not all every time).
  The expanded sentence usually puts the "when" at the front with a comma and keeps the kernel's
  subject and verb. Kernel: "The surfer paddled out." Expanded: "At sunrise, the surfer paddled out
  past the break at Bondi to catch the first wave."
- **Appositives.** A noun or noun phrase beside another noun that renames or explains it, set off
  by commas, removable without breaking the sentence. "The platypus, a curious egg-laying mammal,
  is found only in Australia."
- **Sentence Combining.** Merge short sentences using pronouns, conjunctions and appositives.
  "The bushfire destroyed the shed. The bushfire also destroyed the fence." becomes "The bushfire,
  a fierce blaze fanned by strong winds, destroyed both the shed and the fence."
- **Transitions.** Four categories in TWR's own pacing guide: time and sequence (first, next,
  meanwhile, eventually), illustration (for example, specifically, such as), change of direction
  (however, on the other hand), conclusion (therefore, in short, as a result).
- **Note-taking.** Underline key words and phrases, convert notes to sentences and sentences to
  notes, using the symbols + = -> / and abbreviations.

## 3. Paragraph and composition tools

- **Single-Paragraph Outline (SPO).** Topic sentence (T.S.), three or four dot-point details in
  note form, concluding sentence (C.S.) that echoes the T.S. in a different form. The scaffold
  moves from telling a T.S. from details, to sequencing and selecting details, to writing a T.S.
  from a topic, to writing a C.S. independently. T.S. and C.S. are practised with appositives,
  subordinating conjunctions and the sentence types.
- **Four outlines.** SPO, then the Pre-Transition Outline (PTO) and Transition Outline (TO) for
  students ready for more than one paragraph but not yet for full introductions and conclusions
  (no thesis, single concluding sentence), then the Multiple-Paragraph Outline (MPO) with an
  introduction, thesis and conclusion (3, 4 and 5 paragraph forms).
- **Introductions and conclusions.** GST: general statement, specific statement, thesis. TSG is
  its mirror for the conclusion.
- **Paragraph types** named in the pacing guide: narrative, expository, compare and contrast,
  problem and solution, opinion or argumentative (including pro and con).
- **Unelaborated Paragraph.** A correct but bare paragraph that students revise with strategies
  they already know (transitions, expansion, varied vocabulary, better T.S. and C.S.). Explicitly
  not an error-hunting task. Release goes whole class, then peers with directions, then peers
  without, then the student's own work.

## 4. Revising and editing

Revising (structural improvement) comes before editing (mechanics). Revision is practised first on
unelaborated paragraphs so students are not attached to the text, with named moves: vary
vocabulary, improve T.S. and C.S., and the sentence tools (combining, expanding, transitions)
applied to a draft. TWR publishes a student revise-and-edit checklist and proofreading symbols.
Feedback moves from teacher-modelled whole-class revision to specific, directed feedback, then to
self-revision without prompts, in keeping with principle 6.

## 5. Year-band guidance

Separate pacing guides exist for K-2, 3-5, 6-8 and 9-12, each assuming the earlier band.

- **K-2 (about Years F to 2):** oral rehearsal before writing, capitals and full stops, sentences
  versus fragments, the four sentence types, because/but/so, with heavy modelling.
- **3-5 (Years 3 to 5):** the full sentence sequence (subordinating conjunctions, appositives,
  combining, transitions), note-taking, the SPO across narrative and expository paragraphs, then
  compare-contrast, problem-solution and opinion structures.
- **6+ (Year 6 up):** the same toolkit on harder material, plus PTO and TO into the MPO, thesis
  statements, GST and TSG, multi-paragraph argument.

## 6. The 2024 second edition (2.0)

Adds the PTO and TO outlines, reorganises the sentence-level sequence (transitions framed as
sentence-level work), more student-facing examples, two new multiple-paragraph outlines, expanded
discussion of assessment, and study-group questions. The six principles are unchanged.

## 7. Australian context

Adoption is real but informal (no confirmed Australian office or licensed partner). TWR has run an
Australia-specific webinar and prices its tools in AUD. Think Forward Educators published "The
Writing Revolution in an Australian context" (2022, Templestowe Heights Primary, Victoria), where
TWR thinking sits inside a local writing framework and Comparative Judgement is used for
assessment. Queenwood (NSW) cites the book in its school-wide writing approach and links
sentence-level and cohesion work to NAPLAN results. Other schools publicly describing Hochman
Method work include Matthew Flinders Anglican College (Qld), Mount Stromlo High School (ACT),
Richmond High School (Vic) and Cranbourne Primary School (Vic). AERO's analysis of a decade of
NAPLAN writing data and its explicit-writing trials reflect the same premise without the brand.
One Australian review (Nomanis) notes the evaluation evidence is mixed and warns against
sentence drills crowding out paragraph and genre work.

## 8. Assessment

TWR offers its own tools behind training or membership: beginning, middle and end-of-year
diagnostics (grades 1-2 and 3-12), a Paragraph Evaluation Tool, a Whole-Class Evaluation Tool, a
Single Paragraph Rubric (craft, not content knowledge) and a GST/TSG checklist. These line up
well with the NAPLAN criteria for sentence structure, cohesion, paragraphing and vocabulary, and
less with whole-piece audience, ideas and text structure. Australian TWR schools pair TWR with a
separate holistic rubric or Comparative Judgement rather than relying on TWR's rubric alone.

## 9. Classroom vocabulary a Year 3 to 6 child would know

kernel sentence, fragment, sentence combining, appositive, subordinating conjunction, transition
word, T.S., C.S., SPO, TO, MPO, GST, TSG, thesis statement, unelaborated paragraph, the note
symbols + = -> /. Older students: PTO and the paragraph-type names.
