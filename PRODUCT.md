# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: students in Years 1 to 6 at an Australian primary school, using shared class iPads in a classroom, usually right after finishing a piece of handwriting in their books. Reading ability ranges from early decoding (Year 1) to fluent (Year 6), so the interface must be readable by a six-year-old without feeling little-kid to a twelve-year-old. Secondary: their teacher, who sets device defaults and helps younger students check the typed-out text.

## Product Purpose

The app photographs a student's handwritten writing, tidies it into a clear scan, reads it, and gives warm, specific, curriculum-anchored feedback (stars and a wish) matched to the student's year level. It exists so students get useful formative feedback in the moment, not days later, while keeping every photo and word unstored and private. Success: a student can go from photo to feedback they can read and act on in under a minute, unaided from Year 3 up.

## Positioning

The only writing tool that gives instant, kind, Australian-Curriculum-true feedback on the handwriting already in the student's book, and remembers nothing.

## Brand Personality

Bright, energetic, fun. The energy of a classroom celebration, not a quiet library. Feedback moments should feel like a small event. Voice stays warm, specific and plain-English, worded so children read it themselves.

## Anti-references

Not babyish or cartoonish (Year 6 must not feel talked down to). Not corporate or office-like (no grey dashboards or form-page energy). Not video-gamey (no points, streaks, leaderboards or competition). Not cluttered (one clear thing per screen; energy comes from colour and motion, not density).

## Design Principles

- One job per screen: pick, snap, check, celebrate. Never two competing actions.
- Bright is not busy: bold colour carries the energy; layouts stay sparse and calm.
- The feedback reveal is the peak moment; spend the delight budget there.
- Readable by the youngest, respected by the oldest: large friendly type without babyish styling.
- Privacy is visible: the app says plainly that nothing is saved.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast throughout (children with low vision use class iPads too). Touch targets at least 44px for developing motor skills. Reduced-motion preference fully respected. Feedback language avoids jargon; transcript text is large with generous line height for beginning readers.
